# Research narration

## Source ledger
For each consequential claim record: source ID, document label, page/section or paragraph, exact supporting passage, and your interpretation. Preserve the source's epistemic status. "We propose", "we observe" and "we prove under assumptions A" are different claims. Source IDs establish traceability, not entailment.

For mathematical talks, record definitions and quantifiers, assumptions, theorem statements, dependency of lemmas, and the gap between a proof sketch and a complete proof. Test simple boundary cases before presenting a novel derivation. A computation is not a general proof.

For empirical work, preserve sample size, split/period, units, baseline, metric direction, uncertainty and known limitations. Recompute transformations. Do not invent chart data by reading a trend approximately unless the result is explicitly labeled as digitized/approximate.

For idea documents, distinguish proposed experiments and expected mechanisms from completed results. Empty data is a reason to design an experiment slide, not invent a result.

## Storyboard
Write a short table with slide purpose, claim/topic, source IDs, evidence visual, spoken explanation and approximate time. This is a planning artifact, not slide copy.

Include section membership/question, academic composition, equation representation and reveal sequence where relevant. Separate design-reference IDs from scientific-source IDs: another field's beautiful slide is not evidence for this paper. The optional machine-readable contract is in [plan-check.md](plan-check.md).

Select a narrative suitable to the talk, not a mandatory IMRaD outline. A proof talk may center on an obstruction and construction. A proposal needs feasibility and decisive tests. A short seminar may explain only one result well.

Keep important qualifications on the same slide as the claim they constrain. Put detailed derivations and full source passages in notes if the audience does not need them on-screen. Preserve essential requested material even under a tight slide count.

## Language
Use direct topic titles for setup and supported finding titles for results. Avoid inflated novelty, generic slogans, repeated three-card layouts and ritual "background / importance / outlook" filler. Speaker notes should help a human explain the argument, not narrate the slide-generation process.
