# Academic page patterns

Derived from inspected research presentations and mathematical teaching in [reference-studies.md](reference-studies.md). Select by argument, not a fixed sequence. A pattern is a relationship between evidence and explanation, not a stock graphic.

## Title and research identity

Show the complete research subject, presenter, affiliation and occasion when supplied. Use a small authentic logo only if authorized. Make the title readable in natural lines. A striking constant alone is not an informative research title. Do not invent supervisors, affiliations or credentials. Use the user's public naming preference.

## Outline and section contract

Name actual scientific work: for example “Zero counting conventions”, “Finite matrix construction”, “Trace estimates and limitations”. State the question each section answers rather than generic “background / innovation / future”. Indicate the active section in a divider, short outline reprise, or understated running header. Do not repeat the complete outline on every slide.

A chapter's purpose is separate from its graphical treatment. One section may need ten proof pages, another two figures. Do not allocate equal pages or a separate color just for symmetry.

## Definition plus concrete instance

Place the definition and domain close to a small accurate example. Chen p.8 pairs red unknown matrix entries with the observed-index operator. The example explains the notation immediately.

For zero counting, distinguish multiplicity, distinct locations and simple zeros before showing a ratio. Label synthetic examples. Keep notation beside the example, not solely in a distant glossary.

## Model plus equations

Balance defining equations against an explanatory model, adjusting the split to actual complexity. Chen pp.14–15 preserve a probability graph while changing the inference target. Graph and equation labels denote the same objects.

Use the field's actual object: commutative diagram, geometry, matrix blocks, domain decomposition or dependency graph. If no meaningful diagram exists, use an aligned derivation. Never manufacture a scientific-looking mechanism.

## Stable construction sequence

Chen pp.17–18 keeps the observed series in place, then introduces factorization and a latent sequence below. Peyre pp.4–6 links one assignment across geometry, graph and cost matrix. These are spatial explanations of a mathematical change.

Preserve a base composition across steps. Highlight the changing term or relation. Show assumptions before using them and retain scope conditions in the final state. Avoid moving every equation between frames or treating arbitrary fades as proof animation.

## Theorem and proof sketch

Make assumptions, quantified conclusion, and main proof idea identifiable. A theorem can occupy a quiet, lightly emphasized area with its construction or crucial inequality below. Align relations and annotate the pivotal step. Explain what each lemma supplies. Preserve remainder terms and boundary conditions.

A proof need not fit on one page. Move long algebra to an appendix only when consistent with the request. Call a sketch a sketch. Attribute unverified breakthrough claims instead of confirming them through assertive titles.

## Experimental evidence

Pair a readable chart/table with the experimental conditions and a defensible interpretation. Preserve metric direction, units, baseline, window/sample and uncertainty. Annotate the scientific comparison. A table may be better for exact certificates than a chart when the exact values constitute the argument.

Do not shrink a full journal table until it fits. Select a justified comparison with omitted scope documented, use more slides, or provide an appendix. Never silently drop requested rows. Finite numerical checks cannot establish an asymptotic theorem.

## Synthesis and boundary

Return to the opening question: which claim follows, under which assumptions, and what remains unresolved? Distinguish theorem, evidence, conjecture and proposed experiment. Avoid inspirational filler or a large final statistic without scope.

## Recognizable failures

- Cover, outline, theorem and data pages share a title-plus-three-lines skeleton.
- More colors/fonts/lines/empty space appear, but the scientific structure stays unchanged.
- Prose describes a matrix or correspondence that the audience never sees.
- Giant chapter numbers consume space needed by formulas.
- Pages are sparse because assumptions were removed, or dense because no selection was made.
- Manually spaced formulas break brackets, baselines or subscripts on export.

Check the representative sample for these failures before expanding. Repeat a layout when it helps a proof or controlled comparison; do not vary layouts to meet a quota.
